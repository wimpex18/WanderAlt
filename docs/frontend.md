# Front end and design system

Plain HTML pages at the repo root, one `.js` renderer each, one stylesheet (`wa.css`). No framework and no build step. The direction, what users said and what is open are in `docs/design-brief.md`.

Shared: `i18n.js` and `lang/` (`WA.Lang`: the interface language, Languages below), `moods.js` (`WA.Moods`: what you are in the mood for), `tabbar.js` (the glass drop: the tab bar's, and `WA.glassDrop` for the Map's Show control), `render.js` (`WA.R`: rows, cards, place rows, shelves, section heads, skeletons, empty states, areas, kinds, the "why" tag, interests, last visit), `icons.js` (`WA.Icon` UI icons, `WA.Picto` the Labels discs), `finder.js` (the Where / When / What sheet from the desktop search pill; on phones the pill is a real field that sends its text to the Programme), `ask.js` (`WA.Ask`: reads a search sentence into filters, in English, Estonian and Russian, places included: records, books, thrift, galleries, cinemas, clubs, bars, theatres, museums, craft beer), `route.js` (`WA.Route`: the evening). Data and state: `supabase.js`, `when.js`, `geo.js`, `hours.js`, `bookmark.js`, `lists.js`, `follow.js`, `report.js`, `seen.js`, `auth.js`, `theme.js`, `offline.js`, `install.js`, `sw.js`.

`supabase.js` loads `catalogue_redirects` alongside the catalogue. Retained event/venue ids resolve to canonical records in detail and map links, saves and list contents; raw saved ids remain stored so an admin undo is reversible. Saved looks up records absent from recommendations before declaring them gone, including unverified venues and archived events. Failed lookups retain the save and offer a retry; cached empty answers cannot establish absence. Detail offers the same recovery on lookup failure. Paged results are unique by id and use stable ordering. Tonight claims displayed ids across shelves, so tomorrow's cards do not repeat under Later this week. Separate dated performances keep their own ids.

## System

- **Surfaces.** White paper (`--paper`) over a soft daybreak gradient fixed behind the page (`--daybreak`, `body::before`: peach, lilac, apricot at low strength), a quiet fill (`--paper-2`), ink `#24222c` for type only. The dark theme (`data-theme="dusk"`) is a violet-blue gradient with `#262347` paper and glass over it, never flat black. Never cream, and **no black surfaces**: selection is tinted glass with a vermilion ring (`--select-bg`, `--select-ring`), the one primary action is the vermilion gradient (`--primary-bg`, `--primary-glow`), and map clusters, toasts, offline bar and cancelled flags are tinted too. Text on a tint stays ink; white only on the gradient.
- **One accent**, Tallinn vermilion `--accent` `#d83a14`: the primary key (Tickets, Search), "now", the day-strip bars, the open ring on venue pins. White text on it is 4.7:1. Text in the accent uses `--accent-ink`.
- **Liquid Glass is for what floats over content**, never for content: the top bar, the floating tab bar and its drop, map controls and the Show control, the finder sheet, every sheet's panel, the ticket bar, the toast, the route card (a card of soft gradient the glass bends). The card time badge is solid paper. One recipe (`--glass-*` tokens); `prefers-reduced-transparency` and browsers without `backdrop-filter` get solid paper.
- **Type.** Geologica (variable 300–800; Latin, Latin Extended, Cyrillic, self-hosted in `fonts/`), weights 500–700, tight tracking on headings. Geist Mono only for feed URLs.
- **Icons, two tiers.** UI icons: 24px, a 1.8 line in two tones, drawn the way a system symbol is drawn hierarchically: the line in `currentColor` and, where a shape has a body, that body (`class="t"`) filled at 16% (26% in dusk) behind it. Selected (the current tab, a pressed key, `.wa-ic--solid`) the body fills and the inner lines (`class="k"`) are cut out of it in `--ic-knock`. The static pages carry their icons inline with `data-ic="<name>"`; `npm run build:inline-icons` (`.scripts/sync-icons.js`) rewrites them from `icons.js`, and a test fails when they differ (`WA.Icon`). Labels: round, flat discs, one Tallinn object each on a muted ground with a faint groove ring like a record, for kinds and cities (`WA.Picto`, `.wa-picto--<name>`; the artwork is in `icons.js`, drawn on a 64 grid). They stand in for a picture when there is none and keep their own colours in both themes, because they paint pictures and the accent stays the interface's. The word beside a disc carries the meaning.
- **Shape.** Concentric radii: 8 / 12 / 16 / 20 / 28, pills for chips, search and pins. Cards 20px, sheets 28px.
- **Motion.** `--ease` and `--spring` only; press states scale, sheets rise with the spring. The route card on Now draws itself once, top down (the skyline inks in, the thread grows, nodes drop in, the listing's node pulses once; Another redraws it faster). `prefers-reduced-motion` removes all of it. Map sheet snaps set the final list height once and animate its position; category folding uses grid rows. Tab-pill and grip widths do not animate; the Map's Show pill widens to its option. The Now badge uses a static dot; shelves move only through user input. Temporary loading skeletons shimmer until data arrives. Impeccable exceptions retain the specified spring, Geist Mono feed URLs and the loading shimmer misidentified as a marquee.
- WCAG 2.2 AA; 44px tap floor; focus ring 2px ink.

## Patterns

- **Card**: square photo, glass badge for when ("Tonight · 19:00", "On now · till 23:00"), heart to save, title (two lines), venue · area, walk · price · why tag. No photo: the kind's Label centred on a quiet well.
- **Picture identity**: an event shows its own artwork; without it, its venue's logo or photo (marked "Venue logo" or "Venue photo" on the detail page); without either, its category Label. A venue's logo (`image_source: logo`) is drawn whole on a white tile wherever a venue photo would sit: place rows, map cards, the venue link on an event, saved-list tiles, and, with a credit, the venue detail. Photo retrieval and review rules are in `docs/data.md`.
- **Row** (Programme, venue programme, Saved): rail with time then walk (area when location is off), kind and why tag, English title, venue · area · price, thumbnail. A dated listing without a supplied time says "Time not listed", never claims to run all day.
- **Place row**: photo or Label, name, kind · area, open state (filled dot open, square shut, dashed not filed), walk.
- **Event page**: photo or Label, English title, 1–2 sentence English highlights, explicit performance languages when stated, facts as an icon list (When, Entry, Walk or Area), the ticket bar (price and the ticket seller's host, Tickets or Walk me there), Calendar / Walk there / Save / List, the Going row, the venue card, a native Original description disclosure, Address (mini-map, address, Walking directions), provenance. On phones the tab bar steps aside and a back key appears.
- **Flags**: what the source says about a show, from `flag`. Cancelled and postponed are tinted pills with a strong edge; the card or row greys its photo, strikes the title, never reads "On now", and sorts last on Now's list; the event page opens with a tinted notice and drops tickets, calendar and Going. Sold out and few tickets left are outlined pills; sold out turns Tickets into "Check for returns". Never the accent, which means now.
- **Going** (`going.js`): "I'm going" marks the event in this browser (`wa:going:v1`) and, when signed in, in `going`. The row shows the public count from `going_counts` ("12 going", "You and 11 others"); nobody sees who. Signed out, the mark stays local and a toast offers sign-in. If the count can't be read, the row shows no number.
- **Original description**: closed by default. Opening it fetches at most 2,000 source characters, the original title and a link to the full source. Failed requests offer Try again and retain the source link. Known text languages use `lang`; performance languages come from explicit source statements, never from the announcement's language. English-source excerpts are labelled Source description. English highlights also feed page metadata and social previews.
- **Mini-map**: a still MapLibre map with the pin, in the page's theme; tapping it opens `map.html?pick=<id>`, which centres on that event or place and chooses it.
- **Venue page**: photo or a monogram of the name on its kind's tint, open state, Follow / Walk there / Save, labelled links (Website, Instagram, Facebook), a Next fact (the next show and how many follow), programme by day, the week's hours, Address with the mini-map, provenance. Retained closed venues show a closure notice and never read Open now from old hours.
- **Follows** (`follow.js`) are keyed by identity: `place:<places.id>` for a venue, `src:<handle>` for a source. The stored value is the label You shows. Follows saved as bare names migrate to ids when the catalogue loads (a name shared by two places stays bare). A followed venue or source gets a Calendar link (webcal, the `calendar-feed` function with `place` or `handle`); the calendar app does the notifying, nothing is sent from the site. The source page has its own Follow button.
- **Saved searches**: when Programme has a kind, Free or In English set, a line under the count offers Follow this search and, once followed, Add to calendar. It is a `search:` follow (see `docs/data.md`) and appears on You.
- **Inbox** (`inbox.js`, You, signed in only): the notes the alert job wrote for the account, newest first, unread ones with an accent dot; opening You marks them read and clears the dot on the You button (`data-unread`). Clear inbox deletes them. Signed out, the section says what signing in gives.
- **Notifications** (`push.js`, You, section `#notifications`): optional. The section opens with a state line (Off, On for this device, Blocked, or Needs the Home Screen app), then "Notifications on this device" and "Tonight at places you follow", and when on, "Send a test notification" (made on the device, nothing from a server). Asking for permission happens on the tap. Hidden until the VAPID key is set. On an iPhone in a tab the section offers Add to Home Screen, which opens the install sheet; Blocked inside the installed app points to Settings > Notifications. `sw.js` shows the notification, opens a page on this site, and sets the Home Screen badge to the notifications still showing; `inbox.js` sets it to the unread count whenever a page opens or the count changes (Badging API, installed apps only).
- **Home Screen invitation** (`install.js`, `WA.Install`): iPhone and iPad only send web notifications from an installed app, and few know Share > Add to Home Screen exists. Three parts. The nudge is a glass capsule above the tab bar ("Make it an app"), shown **once, ever** (`shown` in `wa:install:v1`), on Tonight, Programme or Saved, after about 75 seconds of time spent looking at the app (counted across pages in the session), at a calm moment (no sheet, toast or focused field); dismissing it does not bring it back. A quiet entry stays for anyone who wants it: a text-weight "Add to Home Screen" line under the Tonight actions and a slim row near the top of You (`WA.Install.canOffer()`; hidden once installed, in in-app browsers, and where the browser cannot install). The sheet has three animated steps and a tile that pops into a grid, with platform wording: iPhone Safari (menu beside the address bar, then Share), iPad (Share at the top), Samsung Internet (menu, Add page to, Home screen) and other Android browsers (menu, Install app); Chrome and Edge use their own prompt, which Samsung 27+ does not fire. It warns a signed-out reader with saves that the installed iPhone app starts empty (iOS keeps its storage apart from Safari), and says "open in your browser" inside in-app browsers. The benefit line is one sentence per platform (`benefit()`), not a feature list. Inside the installed app, a signed-in reader who has not allowed notifications gets one invitation ("Alerts when plans change") that opens You at `#notifications`. The manifest carries `id`, `scope`, `categories` and three shortcuts; every page sets `apple-mobile-web-app-title`.
- **Staying fresh** (`offline.js`): a page reloads itself when the Tallinn day has turned since it loaded, or when it comes back after more than 30 minutes out of sight (tab switch, app resume, back-forward restore), but never while a sheet is open or a field has focus. It also asks the service worker to check for a new deploy each time. Sessions are not time-boxed: the refresh token keeps a reader signed in until they sign out (Supabase's session time-box and inactivity limits are paid-plan settings and are off).
- **Sign-in** (`auth.js`): one sheet for new and returning readers, "Sign in or create an account": Google, or an emailed sign-in link (`/auth/v1/otp`, which creates the account on first use). The email link returns to the page with the session in the hash, as before. A reader whose email also carries a code (the Supabase Magic Link template must include `{{ .Token }}`; the default one does not) can tap "The email has a code" and type it (`/auth/v1/verify`). Email and password stays one step away ("Use a password instead", with Forgot password and Create account). Sessions last an hour; the refresh token is stored and renews them before they lapse, a second tab's sign-in or sign-out is picked up, sign-out revokes on the server, and a refused sign-in is shown on You. Sign-out first drops this device's push subscription, so a shared device does not keep the last reader's alerts. You also offers "Delete account" (calls the `delete-account` function, then wipes the device) and "Forget everything on this device" (every `wa:` and `wanderalt:` key plus the service worker's caches). There is no sign-in button in the page header; sign-in lives on You, and a password-recovery link opens the new-password sheet on whatever page it lands on. There is no phone sign-in (Supabase needs a paid SMS provider) and no Sign in with Apple (it needs a paid developer account); both break the free-only rule.
- Follows also sync to the account (`follows`), like saves and going. The alert job and the inbox are described in `docs/data.md`; email is dormant and has no switches on You.
- **Flag a problem** (`report.js`): a disclosure under "Where this came from" on an event page. One of five fixed reasons and an optional note of at most 280 characters, inserted into `problem_reports` (insert-only). `/review` lists the open ones with Fixed and Dismiss.
- **Measure from** (Programme filters): a place we hold, picked from a list, saved as `wa:anchor:v1`. It stands in for the device position in `WA.Geo` (`anchor`, `setAnchor`), so walking times and the distance slider work with no location prompt. Clearing the box returns to the device position.
- **Tab bar** (phones): three tabs: Now (a clock with the night spark; the first tab, so it reads right at noon as at midnight), Map, Saved, in a capsule sized to them (at most 348px, centred) whose columns follow the number of tabs, so no slot is ever empty. A vermilion-tinted pill and the icon drawn solid mark the current tab; the Programme and the Guide are not tabs (Now's Events and Places, search and filters lead there, and Now stays marked on them). The Guide stopped being a tab on 4 October: its list and Now's Worth the walk were the same picked places, and Worth the walk sat under the whole day's list; Places on Now replaces both. You is the avatar in the top bar. (`WA.glassDrop`, in `tabbar.js`, is the one implementation; the Map's Show control uses it too.) Press the bar (hold or start sliding) and it swells slightly while a drop of clear glass, taller and wider than a tab, lifts under the finger. The drop carries a magnified vermilion copy of the tabs clipped to its shape, so a tab half under it is half tinted. Letting go settles the drop on the nearest tab and opens it; a quick tap just follows the link.
- **Search field** (Now, phones): a real `type=search` input in the pill, with a vermilion submit key. Enter sends the words to `discover.html?q=`, where the ask field reads a sentence as well as a title; empty goes to the Programme with its field focused. From 1024 the pill is Where / When / What slots that open the finder sheet.
- **Near me** (Now): a switch under the headline beside the mood key, in the accent's tint, kept in `wa:near:v1`. Turning it on asks for the location on the tap (nothing prompts on load); then everything walks from you: the route's first stop is at most 15 minutes away and routes come in steps of five minutes on foot from you, the card opens with "N min walk from you" (or "Right by you") on its spine, the day's list is nearest first with "Nearest first, walking from you" over it, and Places lists picked places closest to you first. Pressed it is tinted with a tick; tapping again returns to time order. A denial leaves "Location is off" and a line on where to allow it. Whenever the location is known, a route card shows the walk from you to its first stop. Walking times print bold with the walk icon on rows and place rows. Other pages keep `R.locPrompt`.
- **Rows that scroll sideways** (the chip rows on Programme, the Guide and Now's kinds; a row drawn after load calls `WA.UI.edges()`): the edge with more behind it fades out over 44px (30px on the kind row, whose half-visible tile must stay legible), and the fade follows the scroll (right at the start, both edges in the middle, left at the end; none when the row fits). `ui-helpers.js` sets `is-more-start` and `is-more-end` from the scroll position and size; `wa.css` draws the fade with a mask on `--fade-l` and `--fade-r`. A cut-off item reads as "more this way" without an arrow or a gradient overlay, so it works on any background, in both themes.
- **Light / Dark button** (top bar, every page, left of You): a bare 42px line icon with no disc, showing the mode a tap switches to (the sun while dark, the moon while light). One tap sets an explicit Light or Dark (`WA.Theme.set`, built in `theme.js` so no page repeats the markup); Auto stays on You under Appearance. Its label names the same mode.
- **Mood key and sheet** (`moods.js`, `home.js`): one glass key under Now's headline reads "Any mood · any price" (or "Records, Jazz · up to €20", "Art & film +2 · free"; tinted once anything is set, cut with an ellipsis so Near me keeps its place) and opens "What's the mood?": at most six moods as one list of glass rows, each with its Label disc, a name, a one-line hint and a round tick, under one line, "Pick one or a few. None means anything." Several may be chosen; none is anything, so there is no Anything tile. A chosen mood opens its narrower picks as chips under it, all optional (Art & film: Art, Film, Stage, Comedy; Live music: Jazz, Indie & rock, Classical; Records & books: Records, Books, Thrift, Markets; Workshops & talks: Workshops, Talks, Easy alone); with none picked the mood means all of it. Then Tickets as a segmented control (Free, Up to €20, Any) with "Shows with no listed price stay in.", and Clear beside the vermilion button. The moods: **Art & film**, **Live music**, **Club nights** (20:00 to 05:00), **Records & books**, **Workshops & talks**, **Craft beer** (12:00 to 02:00; the `taproom` kind, which only the city's `craft_beer` list or a row added by hand makes, `docs/data.md`). A mood is a lens on kinds we already hold (listing kinds, place kinds, a pipeline tag where one exists; a genre pick reads its tags on gigs only); it appears only when the city has at least three upcoming listings plus picked places behind it and only at the hours it suits (`min` per city, `from`/`until` per mood), and a sub only when something is behind it. A mood the hour does not offer stays chosen and waits. Easy alone is the `easy-alone` tag the pipeline writes from a title or venue naming a quiz, a game night, a craft club, an open stage, a drawing night, a language night or a night for meeting people (`docs/data.md`); a row carries an Easy alone tag, and the event page says why with "This is our reading of the listing; the organiser has not confirmed it." The choice (`{ moods, subs, cap }`) is kept in `wa:mood:v1` (an older single `mood` still reads) and narrows both the route and the list under it. A price limit leaves out a listing priced above it, keeps one with no price listed; places carry no price, so only tickets count.
- **Kinds on Now** (`home.js`): under the day tabs, one scrolling row of the kinds in that day's list with their Labels and counts, most first (Gigs, Club nights, Film, Stage, Comedy, Art, Workshops, Talks, Festivals, Markets; Comedy read from tags), shown when the list holds two kinds or more. A chip narrows the list; tapped again it clears. The list is a preview: the next five in tighter rows (60 px picture, titles to two lines), then "Show N more" opens the rest in place (up to thirty) beside "All N", the Programme link, which carries the day, the kind and Near me. With a kind chosen, all of that kind shows.
- **Sheets follow the finger** (`ui-helpers.js`): on a phone every `.wa-sheet` drags down from its grip and head, and from its body when the body is scrolled to the top; let go past a third of the panel (at most 160 px) or with a flick and it closes, otherwise it springs back; upwards it gives a little. Closing goes through a cancelable `cancel` event, as Escape does, so a confirm hears it as Cancel. A tap on the backdrop closes any sheet.
- **Map**: one row of controls, Show (All · Events · Places) and When, both 44px glass. Show is the tab bar's slider in small: a vermilion-tinted pill under the current option, and pressing the capsule lifts the glass drop, which picks the option it settles on; a tap still just presses. Events are pills with the start time (vermilion when on now); places are round pins with their Label, ringed when open now. The places layer is what is open now plus the rooms hosting an event in the chosen window. Pins cluster in screen space; the chosen pin never clusters. Things at one address (within 12 m: a venue and the show in it, two shows in one hall) cannot be split by zooming, so from zoom 16 groups of two to six fan out around their shared point, each pin its own and tappable; a bigger group (a cinema's week) stays a count, and tapping it zooms to street level, then opens the list sheet. Choosing a pin raises a row of cards for what is in view (in the sheet's order); swiping the row moves the choice from pin to pin, and the map slides only when that pin is hidden. Tapping bare map closes the cards. On phones the sheet drags between peek, half and full (a flick goes one stop on); the list drags it too when it cannot scroll that way itself. The locate key rides above the sheet and hides when the sheet is full or cards are up. `?show=events|places` keeps the Show choice; `?pick=<id>` opens on one event or place.
- **Map style**: `map-style.json` (day) and `map-style-dusk.json` (night) are generated by `npm run build:map-styles` from one layer list and two palettes in `.scripts/build-map-styles.js`; edit the palette there, not the JSON. Tiles and fonts are OpenFreeMap. Colour carries the city (green parks, blue water, warm main roads, dashed footpaths); there are no POI icons, because our pins are the points of interest.
- **Evenings in search** (`programme.js`, `route.js`): a plan in plain words ("plan my Friday evening", "what to do tonight", "план на вечер", "õhtu plaan"; `WA.Ask.places(q).plan`) puts an Evenings block above the listings: the stored routes that still hold for the day named (today when none), else one worked out in the page; the cards are the route cards from Tonight. The model, when it is asked, may also say `intent: evening`, `placeKinds` and `openNow`; its place kinds add the Places block (open ones only when asked) and an intent of places narrows the listings to those places. The listings below are always our own.
- **Places in search** (`programme.js`, `ask.js` `WA.Ask.places`): a query that names a kind of place ("records", "bookshop", "plaadipood", "пластинки") puts a Places block of up to four rows, picked ones first, above the listings; a place's name matches too ("terminal"). A shop word with no event or day in it ("vinyl shop", "second-hand") also narrows the listings to events at those places instead of reading as the Market kind. A day or an event word ("record store tonight", "vinyl fair") is a question about listings and shows no block. The place row carries a Picked tag and the pick note.
- **The next few hours** (`route.js`, `route-page.js`, card on Now): `plan({ mood, cap })` returns at most eight routes, best first, in this order: what starts within about two and a half hours (a stored evening that still holds, or one worked out here), then **places on foot** (two or three picked places, each at most ten minutes' walk from the one before, never the same kind twice running, open when you would arrive, daytime kinds before five), then later evenings. An evening is one listing with a start time that has not begun as the anchor; a **picked** place within 15 minutes' walk before it (record shop, bookshop, gallery, thrift, arts centre, cinema) and after it a picked bar, taproom or club within 12 (a bar after four, a club after nine) or, before five, another daytime place. A place before the listing must leave at least half an hour there after the walk. A place whose filed hours say it is shut when you would arrive is left out. A place with no filed hours is tried only inside the hours its kind usually keeps (`USUAL` in `route.js`: shops about 10 to 19, galleries and museums 11 to 18, bars 17 to 01, taprooms 15 to 23, clubs 23 to 04) and still says "hours not filed"; a cinema or theatre with no filed hours is no stop on its own. Places on foot use daytime kinds from 06:00 to 17:00, add bars, taprooms and clubs after that, and try nothing between 03:00 and 06:00, so a walk at midnight never sends anyone to a record shop. A stored route with an unfiled stop outside those hours is not shown. The listing's own venue is never its own before or after. Scores prefer a nearer start, a picked host, a matching interest, a matching mood and, when location is on, closeness to you. Times are worked out here from `now`, the walk (the site's one pace) and the listing's stated or assumed length. Nothing is invented. The pipeline also composes evenings for the next three days with a free model and stores them in `routes` (`docs/models.md`); the page reads them and checks every stop again. A route is its stops, so it lives in the URL (`route.html?s=place:<id>:<minute>,event:<id>:<minute>,…`, validated on read; `&t=<routeId>` carries a stored route's title and note). Its cost is the cheapest price of each listing ("tickets from €9", "price not listed", "free"). The card on Now is glass over a soft gradient: title, a line (area, length, cost), the stops with their walks, a hand-drawn skyline, Walk it (opens the route page) and Another (the next route, drawn again). The same card, without the buttons, lists More routes on the route page. `nextFrom(entry)` and `fromHere(entry)` serve After this (below).

## Working locally

- **Indexing.** Listing and place pages also get a canonical link and schema.org JSON-LD (`Event` for a listing, a `Place` subtype such as `BarOrPub`, `Museum` or `Store` for a place), with every stranger's text escaped so it cannot leave the script element and only http(s) addresses kept. `functions/sitemap.xml.js` serves `/sitemap.xml`: the static pages, every picked place and every upcoming listing, cached for an hour, falling back to the static `sitemap.xml` when Supabase does not answer. The pages themselves are still drawn by JavaScript; a crawler that runs it sees them, and one that does not gets the title, description, canonical and JSON-LD.
- `npm test` includes fixture-based pipeline tests and browser-script VM regression tests for catalogue identity, saved-state recovery, date handling, service-worker caching and middleware security. `npm run typecheck` checks the pipeline and those tests. Real browser checks remain necessary for layout, focus, maps and deployed Functions.
- Share previews: `functions/_middleware.js` rewrites the OG tags of `/detail?id=` for a listing (from `picks`) and, when no listing has that id, for a Guide place (from `venues`: its name, the pick note or kind and area, its photo when it has one); everything shown comes from a row, never from the query string.
- **Public reads and the edge cache.** `supabase.js` `read()` fetches `picks`, `venues`, `venue_details`, `routes` and `catalogue_redirects` from `/api/rest/<table>?<PostgREST query>` (`functions/api/rest/[table].js`), which asks Supabase once per query and keeps the answer in Cloudflare's cache (5 minutes for listings and places, 10 for routes, an hour for redirects), so the free plan's egress is spent once per query per TTL and not once per visitor. Only those tables, only GET, and nothing about the caller is forwarded. If the Function fails, or on a local server where Functions do not run, the page reads Supabase directly. The service worker keeps the same-origin path for offline like the direct one. Signed-in reads and every write still go to Supabase with the user's own token.
- `_headers` governs static responses; `functions/_middleware.js` also applies the security policy to Function responses. Keep the two policies aligned. A local `npm start` does not run Functions or enforce the deployment CSP; verify those on a Pages preview.
- **Languages** (`i18n.js`, `lang/`, `.scripts/build-lang.py`): the interface is English, Estonian or Russian. English is the source: every template stays in English, and `i18n.js` looks each piece of rendered text up in `lang/et.js` and `lang/ru.js` as it appears (text nodes and the `aria-label`, `title`, `placeholder` and `alt` attributes, through a `MutationObserver` that runs before the browser paints; the page title too). A line of pieces joined by "·" is translated piece by piece. A phrase not in a table stays English, so a gap is never a break. Text from a source (listing titles, venue names, pick notes, descriptions) is not in the tables and is left alone; `data-notranslate` or `translate="no"` keeps an element out. The tables are generated: edit `lang/phrases.tsv` (exact phrases) and `lang/patterns.tsv` (phrases with `{placeholders}`), one English / Estonian / Russian row each, then run `python3 .scripts/build-lang.py`; CI fails if the generated files are stale. A placeholder's first letter says what it may be (n, h, m a number; t a time; d a date as the page prints it; u a host name; anything else any text), so "till {t}" cannot swallow a sentence; a Russian cell may give four plural forms `one|few|many|other` for `{n}`. The language is the browser's, or the choice made on You (`wa:lang:v1`, which reloads the page); `<html lang>` follows it; `render.js` takes weekday and month names from `WA.Lang` (the browser's own Intl names; English keeps its fixed ones). ## Speed

- **Snapshot.** `supabase.js` keeps the last catalogue in `localStorage` (`wa:catalogue:v2`, with a fingerprint of its text, and `wa:catalogue:at`, the time it was last confirmed; trusted for six hours, the pipeline's cadence, and never when that time is ahead of the clock) and draws it at once on a return visit, with `DATA_LIVE` false. Successful fresh responses confirm it; service-worker fallback responses retain their original cache timestamp and never renew the confirmation or become live. Actual event dates in Tallinn take precedence over snapshot labels such as Tonight.
- **Service worker.** Only exact public catalogue paths on the configured backend with the shipped public anon credentials enter the data cache, including catalogue redirects. Signed-in or secret-key requests bypass it. Only successful responses replace cache entries; an upstream HTTP error or network outage retains the last good catalogue. Static revalidation keeps failed responses out of the shell cache.
- **Intl formatters** in `when.js`, `geo.js`, `hours.js` and `render.js` are built once and their answers remembered. Building one per call cost about a second per filter tap on a few hundred listings; keep it that way.
- **Programme** answers a tap in the same frame with the chips, count and summary; the list and panel follow in the next task and repeated taps are drawn once. Markup is written only when it changed (`put`), so an unchanged list keeps its pictures.
- **CSS.** Rows below the fold use `content-visibility: auto`. Cards and day headings carry no live backdrop blur (dozens per screen); glass stays on the few things that float. `touch-action: manipulation` on controls.
- **Map.** MapLibre and the map start with the page, in parallel with the catalogue; the first fit waits for both. Pins are redrawn at most once a frame while the map moves.
- **Navigation.** `speculation.json` (sent as the `Speculation-Rules` header) prefetches the tab bar pages and any same-origin link on hover or press, and prerenders event pages on hover or press. Pages must be safe to prerender: no side effects at load, which is why the last-visit stamp is Tonight only, `detail.js` marks an event seen on `prerenderingchange`, and `brand-reveal.js` stands down while `document.prerendering`. `_headers` sends Early Hints for the home page (stylesheet, font, Supabase connection). Pages preconnect to Supabase; the map also to the tile host.

- **Map loading** (`maplibre-loader.js`, `sw.js`, `offline.js`): MapLibre GL JS 6.11.2 (the current release) and OpenFreeMap (`tiles.openfreemap.org/planet`) are kept. The service worker caches the `.mjs` bundles in `vendor/` like other static files (stale-while-revalidate; they were uncached, and Pages serves them `max-age=0, must-revalidate`, so every Map open paid revalidation round trips on a phone). They are not precached, so first load stays light: the first Map visit fills the cache, and on every other page `offline.js` fetches them once at idle, 3 s after load, unless Data Saver or a 3G-or-slower connection says no. `map.html` loads `maplibre-loader.js` first among the deferred scripts so the bundle import starts without waiting for the other 25, and preloads the OpenFreeMap TileJSON, the slowest serial hop. To confirm the cache on an iPhone: visit Tonight, wait 15 s, go offline, open the Map; the page, bundles and style load (tiles need the network).
- **Tab bar diagnostics** (`tabbar.js`): the bar has been seen floating mid-screen during momentum scrolling on an iPhone Home Screen app, intermittently, and has not been reproduced in the iOS 27 simulator. On phones it is given its own compositing layer. Holding the wordmark for 1.5 s switches on a readout (top right, kept per device in `wa:hud`) of the inner and visual viewport heights, the safe-area inset, and `off`, how far the bar's bottom is from where the layout says it should be (`worst` keeps the largest). Red means more than 2 px. A recording of a bad moment with `off 0` means the layout is right and only the drawing is wrong; a large `off` means the viewport is.
- **Sheets and the keyboard** (`ui-helpers.js`, `wa.css`): on iPhone the on-screen keyboard shrinks the visual viewport but not the layout viewport, so a bottom sheet used to sit under the keys with its password field and button hidden. `ui-helpers.js` publishes the visual viewport as `--vv-top` and `--vv-h` while the keyboard is up; `.wa-sheet` sizes to them and a focused field is scrolled into view. Return on the sign-in email field moves to the password.
- **You** (`you.js`, `wa.css`): a short page. Signed out it opens on one card, "Keep what you find", with Google and "Email me a link"; signed in, on who you are (an initial and the email). Then Your taste (up to three kinds, shown as chips with their Labels; routes lean towards them), Start from (a place we hold, picked from a list, saved as `wa:anchor:v1`: walking times and routes start there instead of at the device position, so a hotel works with no location prompt; clear it to use the location), Inbox and Notifications when signed in, Appearance as three icons (follow my device, light, dark) on the title's row, Add to Home Screen when offered, then Following and Opened earlier as folds (`<details>`, closed unless the reader opened them, kept across redraws), Account when signed in, and Data and privacy as a fold. There are no counts and no milestones. Sheets share one calm footer: a quiet Cancel and a small ink pill on the right. The name sheets (New list, Add to a list) offer four name ideas from `WA.Lists.suggestions()` and create on Return; with no lists yet, Saved shows a small "New list" pill instead of an empty tile.
- **Light motion** (`wa.css`, `view-transition.js`): compositor-only (transform and opacity), no new request, about 0.7 KB gzipped of CSS and one tiny script. The first five rows and four cards of a screen settle in over 260 ms with a 30 ms stagger, starting at 72% opacity so nothing is ever hidden and loading is not held back; this runs only while `html[data-enter]` is set (page start until 1.2 s after the first listings, or 5 s), so a refresh or a filter does not replay it. Rows, cards and places sink a hair under the finger; the Open-now dot breathes three times and rests; section headings ease up as they scroll into view where `animation-timeline: view()` exists (Safari 26+). Everything is off for reduced motion, and the entrance for Data Saver.
- **Runs that began earlier** (`render.js`, `runningSpan`): a listing with a start and an end where the start is before today and the end has not passed (an exhibition, a festival pass) is filed under today in grouped lists, not under the day it started, and its rail reads "29 Sep to 3 Oct" instead of a time. Before this, such listings appeared under a heading in the past.
- **Date-only runs** (`when.js`, `endsAtMs`): an end stored as local midnight with no stated start time means through that whole day (sources write "29 Sep to 3 Oct" inclusively), so the run ends a day later than the stored instant. Without this, a running exhibition vanished on the morning of its last day.
- **About** (`about.html`, `about.js`): separate from You on purpose. You is personal and local; About is the public page that people and the Google consent screen link to (`/about`), so it keeps stable anchors: `#what`, `#calendar-feed`, `#privacy`, `#terms`. It is a headline, a one-line lede, three counts from the live catalogue and four folds (`<details>`, What this is open); a link to an anchor opens its fold (`about.js`). The long privacy note lives only here; You's Data and privacy fold is two lines and links to it.
